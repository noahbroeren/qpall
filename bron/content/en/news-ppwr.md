> **In short**
>
> * The PPWR, Regulation (EU) 2025/40, has applied across the EU since 12 August 2026. Pallets fall under it as transport packaging.
> * From 2030, at least 40% of transport packaging within the EU must be reusable. Between your own sites or within one member state, the figure is 100% in principle.
> * From 2030, packaging must be recyclable in grade A, B or C. Grade C means at least 70%.
> * From 2030, plastic packaging must contain a share of recycled material.
> * Our standard pallets are made from recycled HDPE (2) and PP (5) and are fully recyclable at the end of their life.

## What is the PPWR?

The PPWR (Packaging and Packaging Waste Regulation) is the EU packaging regulation, Regulation (EU) 2025/40. It is part of the European Green Deal and has a clear goal: less packaging waste and more circularity.

The regulation entered into force in February 2025 and has applied since 12 August 2026. Because it is a regulation, the rules apply directly and in the same way in every member state. For an article-by-article overview, see our knowledge base guide [PPWR and plastic pallets](@guide-ppwr).

## Why the PPWR is also about pallets

Pallets, crates and other transport packaging usually stay out of sight of consumers. Yet they account for large material flows. That is why the PPWR names pallets explicitly in the reuse targets for transport packaging.

Three types of requirement matter most for pallets:

* **Reuse.** Transport packaging increasingly has to be reusable and part of a reuse system.
* **Recyclability.** Packaging must be designed so that it can be recycled well.
* **Recycled content.** Plastic packaging must consist partly of recycled material.

### What do recyclability grades A, B and C mean?

From 2030, packaging must be recyclable in grade A, B or C. The grade shows how well packaging can be turned back into usable material after collection and sorting. Grade A is the highest grade. Grade C is the minimum: a recyclability of at least 70%.

## What changes, and when?

The PPWR sets binding targets and deadlines up to 2040. These are the key milestones for transport packaging:

| When | What applies |
|---|---|
| February 2025 | The PPWR enters into force. |
| 12 August 2026 | The PPWR applies across the EU. |
| 12 August 2028 | A harmonised material label follows, once the implementing rules are in place. |
| 1 January 2030 | At least 40% of transport packaging within the EU is reusable and part of a reuse system. |
| 1 January 2030 | Between your own sites, within your group or within one member state, 100% reuse applies in principle, with some exceptions. |
| 2030 | Packaging is recyclable in grade A, B or C. |
| 2030 | Plastic packaging contains 10 to 35% recycled material, depending on the type. |
| 2040 | Target of 70% reusable transport packaging. Plastic packaging contains 25 to 65% recycled material. |

## What does this mean for pallets?

Pallets are the backbone of B2B logistics, yet they are rarely viewed strategically. The PPWR changes that. Your choice of pallet now ties in directly with legal requirements.

### Reusable pallets

For pallet flows within the EU, reuse is becoming the norm. Think of pallets that circulate between your own sites, or pallets in a [pool](@sector-pooling). A plastic pallet lasts for many years and is made to be used again and again. That makes it a good fit for the reuse target.

### One-way pallets and export

Not every pallet comes back. For shipments outside the EU, many companies choose a lightweight [export pallet](@type-export) that stays with the recipient. A one-way pallet like this does not count towards the reuse target. The recyclability requirements do apply, though. Our export pallets are made from nearly 100% recycled plastic and are fully recyclable at the end of their life.

### Wooden pallets

Wooden pallets can be reusable too, especially within a well-managed pool. However, they absorb moisture, can splinter and often need ISPM 15 treatment for export outside the EU. For a side-by-side comparison, read [plastic or wooden pallets?](@guide-wood)

## Is your organisation ready for the PPWR?

These four steps help you map out what the PPWR means for your pallets:

1. **Map your pallet flows.** Which pallets circulate within the EU, which come back and which cross the border just once?
2. **Choose reusable where you can.** For regular flows between your own sites or within one member state, 100% reuse applies in principle from 2030.
3. **Ask for documentation.** Record the declaration of conformity and material composition for each pallet model.
4. **Calculate the cost per trip.** A pallet that makes more trips lowers both costs and packaging waste. It also strengthens your sustainability reporting.
