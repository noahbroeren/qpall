> **In short**
>
> * Yes, you can stack plastic pallets, both empty and loaded.
> * With loaded pallets, the static load of the bottom pallet determines how much can go on top.
> * Only stack pallets of the same size and type, on a flat floor.
> * Empty nestable pallets slide into each other. That way almost three times as many fit in a truck.

Do you store goods in your own warehouse, on an outdoor site or with a logistics service provider? Then sooner or later the question comes up: can plastic pallets actually be stacked?

The short answer is yes. Plastic pallets have the same practical properties as wooden pallets, and they come in many sizes and versions.

## Can plastic pallets be stacked?

Yes, in three ways:

* **Empty pallets on top of each other.** Pallets with runners or feet are stacked on top of each other, up to the maximum stacking height of the model.
* **Empty pallets inside each other.** [Nestable pallets](@type-nestable) have hollow feet. When empty, they slide into each other like plastic cups. That way almost three times as many fit in a truck as pallets with runners.
* **Loaded pallets on top of each other.** This is also called block stacking. Here, the load capacity determines how many pallets you can stack.

## How many loaded pallets can you stack?

That mainly depends on the static load of the bottom pallet: the maximum weight a pallet carries when it stands still on a flat floor. The bottom pallet carries its own load plus the weight of all the loaded pallets above it. That total must stay within the static load.

An example: you stack four pallets that each weigh 800 kg including their load. The bottom pallet then carries its own load and the three pallets above it, 3,200 kg in total. Choose a pallet whose static load is well above that.

For heavy loads, [Heavy pallets](@class-heavy) are the logical choice: they carry up to 7,500 kg static. The exact values are listed for each model. You can read more on our page about [the load capacity of plastic pallets](@about-load).

## How high can you stack?

Even if the weight is within limits, you cannot stack indefinitely high. The higher the stack, the sooner it becomes unstable. How high you can safely stack depends on:

* the stability of the load and its packaging;
* a flat, level floor that can carry the weight;
* the rules in your warehouse, such as the permitted floor load.

For empty pallets, the maximum stacking height of the model applies. In pallet racking, you do not stack pallets on top of each other. There, the racking load counts, and Light pallets are not intended for racking.

## What should you watch out for when stacking?

* **The same size and type.** Only stack pallets of the same size and construction. A large pallet on a smaller one is asking for trouble.
* **A stable base.** A pallet with runners is more stable in storage than a pallet on feet. You can click [runners](@acc-runners) under many nestable pallets without tools.
* **An evenly distributed load.** All load values apply to an evenly distributed load. A point load or an uneven load reduces what the pallet can carry.
* **Heavy at the bottom.** Put the heaviest pallets at the bottom. Do not let the load overhang the edge, and place the pallets exactly on top of each other.
* **A secured load.** Secure the load, for example with stretch film, so that boxes cannot shift.
* **No damaged pallets.** Do not use pallets with cracks or deformations. Even minor damage can lead to failure under load.
* **The temperature.** At low temperatures, plastic becomes stiffer and more sensitive to impact; at high temperatures, it becomes slightly more flexible.

You will find more tips for preventing damage in [9 common mistakes that damage plastic pallets](@news-damage).

## The benefits of plastic in storage

* **Long life.** Plastic pallets last for many years. As a result, they are often cheaper per trip than wood.
* **No splinters or nails.** That is more pleasant to work with and prevents damage to your load.
* **Suitable for outdoor storage.** Plastic does not absorb moisture, does not rot and does not go mouldy.
* **Consistent size and weight.** That makes stacking predictable and also works well in automated warehouses.
