## Static load

The static load is the maximum weight a pallet carries when it stands still on a flat, level floor. Think of floor storage or block stacking. If you stack loaded pallets on top of each other, the bottom pallet carries the weight of all the pallets above it. That total weight must stay within the static load.

[[loadill:static]]

## Dynamic load

The dynamic load is the maximum weight a pallet carries while it is moving, for example on a forklift, a pallet truck or a roller conveyor. Because of acceleration, braking and small shocks, this value is lower than the static load.

[[loadill:dynamic]]

## Racking load

The racking load is the maximum weight a pallet carries in pallet racking without decking, when it rests only on the two beams. This is often the decisive value, and also the lowest. Light pallets are not intended for racking.

[[loadill:racking]]

## How does Q-Pall test the load ratings?

We determine the racking load in a test with 25 kg bags on a conventional pallet racking system: the pallet rests with its runners on the two beams, without a shelf underneath.

We test our pallets with an evenly distributed, stable and homogeneous load of bagged goods, for a predetermined period at an ambient temperature of 20 °C. Different load conditions may lead to different results and must be assessed separately.

## Tips for the right choice

- **Choose with a margin.** Take a pallet whose value is well above the weight of your heaviest load.
- **Watch the distribution.** Point loads and unevenly distributed loads reduce the capacity.
- **Look at the beams.** Place the pallet in the racking with the runners at right angles to the beams, as intended.
- **Temperature matters.** At high temperatures, plastic becomes slightly more flexible. At low temperatures, it becomes stiffer and more sensitive to impacts.
