## A to Z

### Anti-static pallets
Plastic pallets with anti-static additives. These prevent the pallet from becoming statically charged and attracting dust, and reduce the risk of malfunctions in sensitive electronics.

### Block pallet
A sturdy pallet for industrial use that you can pick up with a forklift from all four sides. See [block pallets](@type-block).

### Carbon footprint
The greenhouse gas emissions of a pallet over its entire life cycle (raw material, production and transport), expressed in CO₂ equivalent.

### Click-on runners
Separate runners that you click under a nestable pallet with 9 feet without tools. You transport the pallets nested and make them more stable on site. See [runners](@acc-runners).

### CP pallet
A chemical pallet in one of the nine CP sizes (CP1 to CP9) of the European chemical industry. See [CP pallets](@type-cp).

### Dynamic load
The maximum weight a pallet carries while it is moving, for example on a forklift or pallet truck.

### ESD-safe pallets
Pallets with conductive additives, such as carbon black, that safely dissipate electrostatic discharge. Important where there is a risk of explosion and for sensitive electronics.

### Export pallet
A light pallet for a single trip, often nestable. Plastic export pallets are exempt from ISPM 15. See [export pallets](@type-export).

### Feet
Blocks on the underside of the pallet, usually 9 of them. Pallets with feet are often nestable and can be entered from all sides.

### H1 pallet
A hygienic plastic pallet in Euro pallet size, originally a pool pallet for the meat industry. See [hygienic pallets](@type-hygienic).

### HDPE and PP
The two plastics our standard pallets are made of: high-density polyethylene (HDPE, recycling code 2) and polypropylene (PP). Both are easy to recycle. Read more about code 2 in [recycled PE-HD 2: what is it exactly?](@news-hdpe)

### ISPM 15
The international standard for wooden packaging material in export. Plastic pallets are exempt from it. See [ISPM 15 and export](@guide-ispm).

### Load capacity
The maximum weight a pallet can safely carry. There are three values: static, dynamic and racking load. See [the load capacity of pallets](@about-load).

### Nestable pallets
Pallets that slide into each other when empty, so a stack of empty pallets takes up much less space. See [nestable pallets](@type-nestable).

### Perimeter base
A pallet with a base that runs all the way round: runners on all four sides. Examples are the CP6 and CP9.

### Racking load
The maximum weight a pallet carries in pallet racking, when it rests only on the two beams and without decking.

### Rounded edges
Rounded corners and edges prevent damage to packaging, transport equipment and people when the pallet is handled.

### Runners
The continuous bars on the underside of a pallet. They determine stability and suitability for roller conveyors and racking. There are versions with 3, 5 or 6 runners. A pallet with 6 runners is also said to have a perimeter base.

### Static load
The maximum weight a pallet carries when it stands still on a flat floor, for example in block stacking.

### Top deck
The upper surface of the pallet, on which the load rests. There are two variants. An **open deck** has a grid structure: it is lighter, and moisture and air can pass through. A **closed deck** is flat and solid: it is more hygienic and better for small packaging.

### TPE anti-slip
Thermoplastic elastomer: a rubber-like material that is incorporated into the deck of the pallet for extra grip. See [anti-slip pallets](@type-antislip).
