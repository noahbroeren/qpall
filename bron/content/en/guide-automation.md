## Why automation needs different pallets

In a manual warehouse, a forklift driver corrects a crooked or damaged pallet. In an automated system, nobody does. Every deviation in size, weight or flatness can cause a stoppage, an error or damage.

## Five properties that count

* **Dimensionally stable.** An injection-moulded plastic pallet always has the same size. It does not swell or shrink with moisture.
* **Constant weight.** Important for weighing systems and checks on the line.
* **No nails or splinters.** Nothing catches on rollers, chains or sensors.
* **The right base.** For roller and chain conveyors, choose a pallet with runners, like almost all of our [heavy pallets](@class-heavy): three or six continuous runners give an even running surface. Pallets with feet are less suitable for roller conveyors.
* **Anti-slip.** An [anti-slip deck](@type-antislip) keeps the load in place when accelerating and braking, so you need less stretch film and can run faster.

## Watch the racking load

In a high-bay warehouse, the pallet sits on two beams. So look at the **racking load**, not the static value. Our heavy pallets carry up to 2,000 kg in racking. Read more about [pallet load capacity](@about-load).

## Which pallets suit automation?

* **Heavy pallets on 3 or 6 runners** for high-bay storage, cranes and heavy loads.
* **Medium pallets with anti-slip** for AGVs and fast lines.
* **Closed deck** when sensors need to ‘see’ the pallet or small products must not sag.
