> * An uncontrolled discharge can damage electronics or cause a spark near flammable gases, powders or liquids.
> * ESD-safe pallets are made of plastic with conductive additives, such as carbon black. These dissipate the charge in a controlled way.

Pallets carry all kinds of goods and raw materials. Because loads vary widely, there are different types of pallets. A pallet for fresh products has to meet different requirements than one for electronics or for flammable liquids, powders or gases.

For that last group, there are **ESD pallets**: ESD-safe plastic pallets. What are they, and when do you need them?

## What does ESD mean?

ESD is short for **electrostatic discharge**. Everyone knows the effect. You pull a jumper over your head and hear it crackle: the friction has built up a static charge, which discharges straight away.

Or you get up from a chair, touch a metal door frame and feel a small shock. At that moment, you discharge. It may startle you, but it does no harm.

With sensitive electronics or flammable substances, it is a different story. There, such a small discharge can cause damage.

## Plastic pallets and static charge

Plastic is an insulator by nature: it conducts electrical charge poorly. Friction between different plastics, such as the pallet and the packaging of the load, can create a charge. This stays on the surface until it discharges all at once.

That is why static charge must be dissipated in a controlled way. This is only possible if the pallet material lets the charge flow away.

## The risks of uncontrolled discharge

* **Explosion hazard.** A small spark can ignite flammable gases, powders or liquids.
* **Damage to electronics.** Circuit boards and components can fail immediately. Sometimes the damage is not visible at first and faults only appear later.

Without ESD protection, you risk damaged products or even a dangerous situation. So for ESD-sensitive loads, check carefully which pallet is suitable.

## What makes plastics ESD-safe?

Whether a material holds or dissipates a charge depends on its electrical resistance. The lower the resistance, the better the charge can flow away. Roughly speaking, there are four levels:

* **Insulative.** Ordinary plastic. The charge stays put.
* **Anti-static.** Charge builds up more slowly and flows away gradually.
* **Dissipative.** The charge is drained evenly and in a controlled way.
* **Conductive.** The charge is drained immediately.

Plastic becomes ESD-safe when conductive additives are added, such as carbon black. These lower the resistance, so that the charge flows away through the pallet in a controlled way instead of as a spark. You can find more technical terms in our [glossary](@about-glossary).

## ESD-safe or anti-static?

An anti-static pallet mainly prevents charge from building up and stops the pallet attracting dust. An ESD-safe pallet goes a step further: it actively dissipates charge in a controlled way. Read more about the first type in [anti-static pallets: benefits and how they work](@news-antistatic).

The resistance you need depends on your process and the safety regulations that apply to it. Agree this with your safety adviser.
