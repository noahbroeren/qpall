> **In short**
>
> * SCOPAC stands for *Sustainable Composites based Packaging from Low-Value Waste*.
> * The project combines residual plastic packaging waste with waste fibres to create circular composite materials.
> * Partners included Brightlands Materials Center, Witcom, Eurocarbon and Q-Pall.
> * Q-Pall tested the new materials for suitability and performance in pallets made from recycled plastic.
> * The project was co-funded by the European Union.

## What is SCOPAC?

SCOPAC (*Sustainable Composites based Packaging from Low-Value Waste*) is a collaboration that in 2025 worked on reusing low-value residual streams. These were plastic packaging waste combined with waste fibres. The goal: to develop high-quality, circular composite materials for the packaging and transport industries.

A composite is a material made up of several components, in this case plastic with fibres. The fibres make the plastic stiffer and stronger.

## Who did what?

* **Brightlands Materials Center and Witcom** contributed their technology for recycling fibre-reinforced thermoplastics, with as little loss of quality and as much circularity as possible.
* **Eurocarbon** supplied the waste fibres.
* **Q-Pall** evaluated and tested the new composite materials in pallets made from recycled plastic.

## Q-Pall's role

We already make our standard pallets from almost 100% recycled plastic, a mix of HDPE and PP. Read more about this material in [recycled PE-HD 2: what is it exactly?](@news-hdpe)

In SCOPAC, we investigated whether the new composites can be used in pallets and how they perform in logistics environments. In this way, we helped to map out the potential of a new circular material.

## From waste to raw material

The SCOPAC consortium stood for a new, sustainable value chain. In it, waste is not an end point, but a raw material for high-quality applications. Brightlands Materials Center also worked on scaling up the technology to 200 tonnes a year. The aim was a circular business model that can be scaled up within the Netherlands and Europe.

This fits the way we handle materials. At the end of their life, we take pallets back and grind them into raw material for new pallets. Read more about [recycling plastic pallets](@news-recycling) and our approach to [sustainability](@about-sustainability).
