> **In short**
>
> * HACCP stands for Hazard Analysis and Critical Control Points: a method for controlling food safety risks.
> * Companies that prepare, process, package, transport or sell food are legally required to work this way. That includes storage and transport.
> * HACCP does not prescribe a specific pallet. You decide which pallet suits the risks in your process.
> * Does food come into direct contact with the pallet? Then choose a hygienic pallet made of virgin plastic.
> * Pallets made of recycled plastic are intended for packaged products, not for direct contact with food.

Companies that handle food must do so safely and hygienically. They are responsible for making sure consumers do not get ill from their products. That responsibility also applies to transport and storage.

A plastic pallet can help, but not every pallet suits every application. Below you can read what to look out for.

## What is HACCP?

HACCP stands for *Hazard Analysis and Critical Control Points*. It is a system a company uses to identify and control food safety risks. You determine where in the process things can go wrong, and how you prevent and monitor this.

That is why HACCP rules are also known as food hygiene rules.

## Who has to work to HACCP?

Companies that produce, process or sell food must have and maintain a HACCP system. Examples include:

* meat and fish processors and other food manufacturers
* hospitality businesses, hospitals, care homes and prisons
* butchers, greengrocers, cheese shops and supermarkets
* petrol stations and sports canteens that sell food

In short: wherever food is handled or sold, HACCP rules apply.

## What does HACCP mean for storage and transport?

During transport and storage, raw materials, (semi-)finished products and end products must also be handled properly. The packaging must offer protection, but how you transport and store the products also counts. The aim is to keep safety and health risks as small as possible.

HACCP does not prescribe a specific type of pallet. You look at the risks in your own process and choose a pallet that fits. Useful questions: does the product sit directly on the pallet, how often do you clean the pallets, and in which areas do you use them?

## Why plastic pallets instead of wood?

Wood is porous. It absorbs moisture, can go mouldy and gives pests and insects a place to nest. Wooden pallets can also splinter, which is dangerous for staff and for the products on the pallet.

Plastic is not porous, does not absorb moisture and does not splinter. A plastic pallet is also easy to clean. This gives you better control over the risk of contamination.

## Hygienic pallet or recycled plastic pallet?

We make our standard pallets from recycled plastic. They are a sustainable choice for packaged products, where the pallet does not come into direct contact with the food. They are not intended for direct contact with food.

Does the food sit directly on the pallet? Then choose a [hygienic pallet](@type-hygienic). We make these from virgin (new) plastic, in a colour of their own.

| Situation | Suitable pallet |
|---|---|
| Food packed in boxes, crates or film | Recycled plastic pallet |
| Food directly on the pallet | Hygienic pallet made of virgin plastic |
| Meat industry and other processes with strict hygiene requirements | Hygienic pallet or H1 pallet |

Read more about the right choice for your sector on [plastic pallets for the food industry](@sector-food).

## What makes a pallet hygienic?

A hygienic pallet has a smooth design that is as closed as possible. There are as few spaces and seams as possible where dirt can build up. The pallet does not absorb moisture, can withstand intensive cleaning and has no splinters or nails.

The best-known hygienic pallet is the H1 pallet: a plastic Euro pallet originally developed for the meat industry. Read more in [what exactly are H1 pallets?](@news-h1)
