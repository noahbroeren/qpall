> **In short**
>
> * Many small incidents in logistics can be traced back to the properties of the pallet.
> * Four properties really matter: non-porous, no splinters, a constant weight and a constant size.
> * They mean fewer rejects, less damage, a reliable tare weight and fewer stoppages on automated lines.
> * Plastic pallets also fall outside ISPM 15, the export rules for wooden packaging.

In most organisations, the pallet is the quietest link in the chain. Nobody sets a KPI for it and nobody mentions it in the annual report. It sits under the load, and that is that.

Until something goes wrong. A splinter in a hand or in a product. A wooden board that is just too narrow for an AGV sensor. A load that weighs 3 kilos more on the weighbridge than on the delivery note. A mould stain on a pallet that has just come in from outdoor storage. A customs officer asking for an ISPM 15 mark.

None of these incidents is unrelated to the pallet. They can all be traced back to the properties of the material underneath. Four properties really matter in operations.

## 1. Non-porous

A pallet that does not absorb moisture gives mould and odours no chance to penetrate the material. It is also easy to clean. For food processing, pharma, cold chains and cleanrooms, that is not an advantage but a requirement.

It matters outside those sectors too. A shipment of dry goods on a damp wooden pallet is a complaint in the making. A product on a pallet that has absorbed odours or residues comes back as a return.

Does your load come into direct contact with food, or do you work in a cleanroom? Then choose [hygienic pallets](@type-hygienic) made from virgin plastic. Our recycled plastic pallets are not intended for direct food contact.

**What it delivers:** fewer rejects, fewer returns and fewer discussions with your customer about the state of the pallet.

## 2. No splinters

Splinters seem like a detail. Until someone tears a glove, damages a product or packaging, or blocks a roller conveyor. A pallet without splinters eliminates a whole category of small incidents: the kind that never becomes big, but keeps coming back.

**What it delivers:** less product and packaging damage, fewer safety reports and fewer interruptions on the shop floor.

## 3. The same weight, every time

Wooden pallets vary in weight: per piece, per batch and per season. Anyone who works with tare weights, and that is everyone who invoices or ships by weight, has a constant source of inaccuracy as a result.

A pallet with a constant weight lets your weighing system do exactly what it should. No corrections afterwards, no margins for uncertainty and no difference between delivery note and weighbridge.

**What it delivers:** a reliable tare, predictable freight costs and records that add up.

## 4. The same size, every time

Automation does not like variation. Palletisers, AGVs, conveyors and stacking systems are set to a fixed footprint. A pallet that is 4 millimetres too wide, or that has a broken edge board, is a fault for a sensor.

A pallet with identical, lasting dimensions fits the same template every time. That is the basis for an automated process that runs without manual intervention. Read more in our guide on [pallets for automation](@guide-automation).

**What it delivers:** fewer line stoppages, higher throughput and lower maintenance costs for your installation.

## And then there is ISPM 15

Besides what the pallet does in the process, there is an external requirement where the material makes the difference. For exports outside the EU, wooden packaging material must be treated and marked in accordance with ISPM 15. Plastic pallets fall outside that standard: no paperwork, no mark and no risk of rejection at the border. More on this in [ISPM 15 and export pallets](@guide-ispm).

## In conclusion

A pallet is not the most important part of your logistics chain. But it is part of every transaction: every pallet exchange, every weighing and every automated handling step.

So the question is not which material is better. The question is which properties your process needs, and what it delivers when they are no longer variable.
