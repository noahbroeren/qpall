## Choosing pallets with the environment in mind

When choosing pallets, you first look at what the pallet is needed for and what function it has to fulfil. Then you choose between wood and plastic. That choice is increasingly linked to corporate social responsibility (CSR).

For many companies, the environmental impact of products is now a standard decision criterion, alongside price and quality. That also applies to load carriers such as pallets. Below, you can read where that impact comes from and how you can limit it.

## What is a carbon footprint?

The carbon footprint is a measure of greenhouse gas emissions, of which CO₂ is the most important. CO₂ is mainly released by the use of fossil fuels, for example for transport, electricity and heating.

For a product such as a pallet, all the greenhouse gases needed to make it are converted into kilograms of CO₂ equivalent (kg CO₂e). Most emissions arise from extracting raw materials, from transport and from production.

## Where do a pallet’s emissions come from?

* **Raw material.** Extracting and producing new raw materials takes a lot of energy. We make our standard pallets from nearly 100% recycled plastic, a mix of HDPE and PP. This way, we replace new raw material with plastic that already exists.
* **Production.** When developing new pallets, we apply strict design targets for energy and material efficiency.
* **Transport.** In the pallet industry, most CO₂ emissions come from transport. That is why we produce [locally](@about-local), in six factories spread across Europe. Almost every customer is within 600 kilometres of a Q-Pall factory.
* **End of life.** At the end of their life, our pallets are fully recyclable. The material becomes raw material for new pallets.

## How Q-Pall uses the carbon footprint

Q-Pall is aware of its impact on the environment. We have calculated the carbon footprint of plastic pallets and use the result as a baseline: the starting point for improving the environmental performance of our products. This also plays a major role when we develop new pallets.

Our sustainability performance is also independently assessed by EcoVadis. In October 2026, we again achieved the bronze medal, placing us in the top 35% of assessed companies. Read more on our [sustainability](@about-sustainability) page.

## Reuse is sustainable

Is CSR an important part of how you do business? Then consider used plastic pallets too. Reusing pallets means that as few raw materials as possible are lost.

Through [Q-Used](@used), we offer inspected used plastic pallets in various models. They are up to 40% cheaper than new pallets. Availability changes, so ask us about current stock.

## What the PPWR means

Legislation also calls for more attention to the environment. The European packaging regulation PPWR entered into force in February 2025 and has applied since 12 August 2026. Pallets are transport packaging, so they fall under it too.

From 2030, packaging must be recyclable in class A, B or C, where class C means at least 70%. Q-Pall has a PPWR declaration of conformity for each pallet group; for most models you can download it on the product page. Read more in our guide to [PPWR and plastic pallets](@guide-ppwr).
