> **In short**
>
> * A wooden pallet is usually cheaper to buy, but the price of timber fluctuates strongly.
> * A plastic pallet lasts much longer. As a result, it is often cheaper per trip.
> * Plastic pallets do not absorb moisture, do not splinter and are exempt from ISPM 15.
> * Used plastic pallets are up to 40% cheaper than new ones.

## Wood is cheaper to buy

If you compare [plastic and wooden pallets](@guide-wood), plastic wins on many points. Plastic does not absorb moisture and is easy to clean. It does not splinter and there are no nails that work loose.

Plastic pallets are also often lighter, depending on the type. A nestable plastic Euro pallet weighs just over 5 kg, a wooden Euro pallet around 25 kg. A Heavy plastic pallet weighs 17 to 28 kg, roughly the same as a wooden Euro pallet.

So why are so many wooden pallets still in use? Mainly because of the price. Per pallet, wood is usually cheaper.

## Timber prices fluctuate strongly

The price of wood is not stable. This became very clear during the coronavirus crisis. When the pandemic broke out in early 2020, the timber industry expected prices to fall and cut production.

That expectation turned out to be wrong. Construction carried on at full speed and many people in the United States took up DIY projects. Demand for wood rose sharply, supply lagged behind and timber prices climbed to record levels.

If you use a lot of wooden pallets, you feel price rises like these straight away, because wooden pallets often need replacing. You buy a plastic pallet far less often. That is why the purchase price carries less weight.

## Calculate the cost per trip

A fair comparison is not made per pallet, but per trip. With normal use, a plastic pallet lasts for many years. A wooden pallet often needs repairing or replacing after just a few years.

When comparing, also include the costs that are easily overlooked with wood:

* **Repairs and replacement** of broken boards and loose nails.
* **Damage to loads and machines** caused by splinters, nails and varying dimensions.
* **The ISPM 15 treatment** that wooden pallets need for export.
* **Freight costs**, because a lighter pallet saves on transport.

Divide the total cost by the number of trips. In many cases, plastic then turns out to be the most cost-effective choice. See the from-prices per size and load class in [what does a plastic pallet cost?](@guide-prices)

## Cleaning and export

Because plastic pallets are easy to clean thoroughly, they are widely used by food producers and in the pharmaceutical industry. For applications where hygiene comes first, there are [hygienic pallets](@type-hygienic) made of virgin plastic. Wood is porous and can carry mould.

For export, wood must also be treated in accordance with [ISPM 15](@guide-ispm). This international standard aims to prevent insects and plant diseases from spreading via wooden packaging. The wood is treated, usually with heat, to kill any pests that may be present, and is given a stamp.

Plastic pallets are exempt from ISPM 15. So they need no treatment and no stamp, even when exporting to countries such as the US, China, Australia or the United Kingdom.
